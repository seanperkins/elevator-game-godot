# UI Design — Board and Management

**Date:** 2026-08-02 (revised after multi-model review, rounds 1 and 2)
**Status:** Approved. Implementation plan is the next artifact.
**Relationship to `2026-08-01-elevator-incremental-design.md`:** extends it. §9 of *this*
document lists both the behavioural deltas and the edits that design spec needs.
It also **supersedes the UI tasks** of `plans/2026-08-02-elevator-milestones-1-3.md`
(Tasks 10, 12 and 14), whose `ui/upgrade_panel.gd` deliverable is replaced here.

## 1. What this covers

The screen, at both ends of a run: 6 floors and one shaft, and 40 floors and eight.
It settles where information lives, what each gesture does, and how the layout
degrades as the board fills. It does not touch game rules.

**This is a design spec, not the implementation plan.** Method signatures, error
returns, constant placement and per-task sequencing belong to the plan that
follows. What this document owes that plan is a complete list of the files that
must change and the behaviours that must be tested — §7 and §8 — because review
found twice that an incomplete file list is how a design ships a mirrored board.

Three comparable games shaped it. **SimTower** made elevator management the whole
game and became unwieldy at scale — the failure this design is arranged against.
**Project Highrise** solved that by deleting the mechanic, which is the trap.
**Tiny Tower** is the mobile reference, and its consistent complaint is menu
clutter, which is why Management is one surface rather than a tree. **Elevator
Saga** scores the player on throughput and wait time; those numbers are what
Management leads with. **Mini Metro** is the posture: the board is the UI.

## 2. Two views

**Board** — the building. Dispatch, and every purchase that has a physical place.
**Management** — everything with no place on the board: invisible upgrades,
dispatch policy, prestige, and the numbers that justify them.

One HUD button switches between them. It reads `MANAGE` on the Board and `BOARD`
in Management — never `CLOSE`; a view is not a sheet. The simulation runs in both.
The current half-height sheet (`SHEET_FRACTION` and the panel geometry in
`game/game_root.gd`) is removed.

The button sits top-right, which is the worst reach on the phone. That is
deliberate: a floating button in the thumb zone would overlay the bottom-right of
the board, and at eight shafts the bottom-right of the board *is* a shaft column
— a dispatch target. A permanent tab bar would cost 88 units of board height for
a control used a few times a minute. Top-right is the cheapest correct place.

## 3. The board

### 3.1 Geometry

720x1280 portrait. A 96-unit HUD holds cash, rent/min, combo, the shaft pager and
the view button. The remaining **1184 units are board**.

Floors run **bottom-up**: the lobby is floor 0 at the bottom.

Let `N` = floors built, `G` = 1 while `N < 40` and 0 at the cap (the ghost floor,
§3.4). Then:

```
row_height h = 1184 / (N + G)
floors span  the lower N·h of the board
ghost band   the topmost h, present only while G = 1
```

**The shaft viewport spans the floors only — `N·h`, not the full 1184.** It is
inset from the top by the ghost band. This is load-bearing in three places: it
frees the ghost band for its own tap (§3.4), it keeps `Gesture`'s cancel-edge
arithmetic correct (§4.2), and it means a column's local y-origin is the top
floor rather than a non-floor.

### 3.2 One row↔y transform, used by everything

The row↔y identity currently lives in several separate places, all top-down.
Flipping only the row layout would mirror every dispatch **self-consistently** —
gesture, highlight and car would agree with each other and disagree only with the
floor labels — which looks correct in casual play and in any static screenshot.

So the inversion lives in exactly one place. It has **four** consumers, not three:

| consumer | what it converts |
| --- | --- |
| `sim/gesture.gd` | touch y → floor |
| `view/building_view.gd` | floor → the y a `FloorRow` is placed at |
| `view/shaft_column.gd` | a car's *fractional* row position → y |
| `view/floor_selector.gd` | selected floor → rail marker and bubble y |

`building_view.gd` is the one that draws the floor labels — the reference against
which "the board is mirrored" is even defined. Hand-flipping it while the other
three use the transform reintroduces the round-1 defect one refactor later.

**Two frames.** The transform is **column-local**: y = 0 is the top floor's top
edge, and the column is `N·h` tall. `building_view.gd` works in the **board**
frame, which is offset by the ghost band: `board_y = G·h + local_y`. Both frames
are stated because a naive use of the local transform in the board frame draws
the top floor inside the ghost band and leaves the bottom band empty.

**The mapping is an edge table, not a division.** Band `k` (counting down from the
top of the column) spans `[edges[k], edges[k+1])` where `edges[k] = k·h` for
`k ∈ [0, N]`, computed once when the board is built. Floor `f` occupies band
`k = N-1-f`.

```
floor_to_y(f)  = edges[N-1-f]                       # exact: a stored value
car_y(p)       = (N - 1 - p) · h                    # continuous, p is a float
y_to_floor(y)  = N-1-k, where edges[k] <= y < edges[k+1]   # by comparison
```

Division is avoided deliberately. `y_to_floor(floor_to_y(f))` computed as
`N-1-floor(y/h)` is **not** an identity in IEEE double: at N=29,
`28·h = 1105.0666666666666` and `1105.0666666666666 / 39.46666666666667 =
27.999999999999996`, so floor 0's top edge resolves to floor 1. Twelve of the
forty floor counts have at least one floor that fails this way. Comparing against
the same stored edges makes the round trip exact by construction at every N.

`car_y` is the continuous form and agrees with `floor_to_y` exactly at integer
positions. It exists because cars occupy fractional rows — `set_car_position`
receives values like 2.4 — and coercing that to an integer floor makes a moving
car jump between rows instead of gliding.

**`Gesture` returns floors, not screen bands.** `tests/test_gesture.gd` currently
pins top-down band semantics and is rewritten as part of this change (§8). The
alternative — leaving `Gesture` top-down and converting in `ShaftColumn` — keeps
those tests green while testing the inversion nowhere.

### 3.3 Row anatomy

Four fixed regions, identical on every floor at every board size:

| x range | width | contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–26 | 26 | waiting count, right-aligned, brightest element on the row |
| 30–34 | 4 | tenant bar (three states, below) |
| 38–64 | 26 | floor number, dimmed |
| 64–240 | 176 | people strip |
| 240–720 | 480 | shaft viewport: three columns on a 160-unit pitch |

**The count is leftmost and never moves.** It is the number a dispatch decision is
made on, so it cannot be the element whose position depends on how many shafts the
player owns, and it cannot sit unlabelled beside the floor number where the two
read as one value.

**There is no tenant status text on the board.** The 4-unit bar carries all three
states:

- **tenanted** — filled proportional to satisfaction, red→green
- **moving out** — red, and the fill *drains over the 60-second countdown*, so the
  bar is the countdown rather than labelling one
- **vacant** — solid grey

An earlier draft put `VACANT` / `leaving Ns` in the gutter, where it overlapped the
floor number by ~12 units at the capped row height; moving it into the people strip
merely traded that for overlapping sprites 8–11, and vacant floors still spawn
passengers, so the two co-occur. In the dense tier the text would also sit on the
crowd bar, whose *length* is the encoding. The draining bar has no such collision at
any row height and needs no region of its own. It costs the exact seconds, which
§5.3 of the design spec does not require — only that the countdown be displayed.

A **vacant** floor additionally renders its re-lease price right-aligned at the
strip's right edge (`$40`, or `FREE` when nothing is tenanted), and its sprite cap
drops from 12 to 10 for that floor: sprite 9 ends at x = 204 and the price occupies
[206, 240], so they cannot overlap.

The people strip is a **fixed** 176 units. Sizing it from whatever the columns
leave over means it reaches zero at the shaft cap, which deletes the crowd
exactly when the building is busiest.

**People are squares, packed by one rule everywhere** (`view/chip_grid.gd`):
`rows = floor(sqrt(n))`, `cols = ceil(n / rows)`, each rank centred on the block
so a short last rank is not ragged — 2 side by side, 3 side by side, 4 as 2+2,
5 as 3+2, 8 as 4+4, 12 as 4+4+4. Taking the *ceiling* of the rows instead would
render 8 as 3+3+2, a rank taller and visibly uneven.

The same rule lays out a car's seats, so a passenger looks and packs identically
before and after boarding; only the label changes, from a call arrow to the floor
they pressed. Where the area cannot hold the balanced shape the rule gives way
rather than overflowing: too narrow adds ranks, too short adds columns, and a
space too small for everyone shows what fits, with the exact count still beside
the strip. A space too small for even one square reports **zero** rather than
one — flooring it at one rank is how a 30-unit square ends up drawn inside a
25.6-unit car.

Columns sit on a **160-unit pitch** and are drawn and hit-tested at **156 units —
85pt** at the 0.546 iPhone 15 scale, with a 4-unit gutter between them.

An earlier revision fitted five on a 96-unit pitch, drawn at 92 (50.2pt). That
cleared the 44pt touch floor but left the car 86 units wide, and a car is not
just a target: it holds a rank of seats, each of which must show a two-digit
floor. Four across 86 units is ~11pt per seat, which puts the digits near 6pt at
the iPhone scale — half Apple's floor for body text. Three columns buy 34-unit
seats and ~13pt digits. **The cost is paging sooner: eight shafts is three pages
rather than two**, which is the trade taken deliberately, because a shaft you
cannot read is worse than a shaft you have to page to.

### 3.4 Ghost floor and ghost shaft

**Ghost floor.** While `N < 40`, an empty row of height `h` sits above the top
floor reading `+ BUILD FLOOR $<cost>`. Tapping anywhere in it buys a floor. It is
a row, not a button, so the next floor is always visibly *there*, and at the cap
the `G` term simply leaves the divisor. Because the shaft viewport stops below it
(§3.1), the whole band is tappable — the columns do not cover it.

**Ghost shaft.** The same device applied to columns. Round 1 found that with five
visible slots and five owned shafts, no empty slot is ever on screen again, so
shafts 6, 7 and 8 are unreachable and `Building.MAX_SHAFTS = 8` is dead — while
`tests/test_upgrades.gd` stays green, because it drives `Upgrades` directly.

```
slot_count  = min(owned + 1, MAX_SHAFTS)
max_scroll  = max(slot_count - visible_shafts, 0)      # visible_shafts = 3
```

At `owned = 3` that is 4 slots and `max_scroll = 1`, so paging right reveals the
buyable slot. At `owned = 8` there is no trailing slot and the pager caps at 5.

**All three visible positions still draw a placeholder; only the trailing one is
buyable.** The existing placeholders exist so the early board reads as room to
grow rather than as void, and that stays. The trailing slot — index `owned` —
shows its price and takes the tap; the placeholders beyond it are inert, unpriced,
and visually flatter. Without this rule several identical-looking empty slots
would be on screen with only one live.

### 3.5 Density tiers

Above a **40-unit row height** the people strip draws individual sprites, coloured
green→red by remaining patience, capped at 12 (10 on a vacant floor, §3.3) with the
count carrying the overflow.

At or below 40 units the sprites collapse into a **single crowd bar**: its colour
is the *worst* patience on that floor, and its length is the crowd measured against
the same 12-passenger cap, saturating at full width beyond that. A floor with 12
waiting and one with 40 therefore draw the same bar — the count beside it is what
distinguishes them, and the count is always exact.

With the ghost floor in the divisor the tier changes at 29 floors
(`1184/29 = 40.83` → sprites; `1184/30 = 39.47` → bar). The threshold is a
legibility choice, not a geometric floor: a 10×14 sprite still fits a 29.6-unit
row with 7.8 units of margin. It is a tunable constant.

**This replaces spec §8.5's crowd-bar trigger; it does not add a tier.** §8.5
triggers the bar on `waiting > 12` and its node budget counts sprites and crowd
bars as coexisting. Here they are mutually exclusive, which changes the budget
substantially — see §9.

The alternative — keeping rows tall enough for sprites and scrolling the board
vertically — was rejected. Dispatch is an absolute drag onto a floor's band, so a
vertically scrolled board can only dispatch to floors currently on screen, and
"any floor is one short drag away" is the property §2.1's whole input model rests
on. **The board never scrolls vertically.**

### 3.6 Shaft paging

The shaft viewport shows three columns and pages horizontally with `<` `>` controls
in the HUD. The pager hides itself only when every slot — including the trailing
ghost shaft — fits. Buying a shaft pages to it. The label counts *shafts*, not
slots, so the ghost slot is excluded from its totals; its exact format is the
implementation plan's to settle.

Paging is a **tap, never a swipe**. §2.1 establishes that a vertical thumb drag
arcs sideways by more than half a column, so any horizontal reading of motion on
the board would steal the primary verb.

Columns paged out of view are **hidden**, not merely clipped. An earlier draft
justified this by asserting that `clip_contents` suppresses drawing but not
hit-testing; **that claim is probably false** — Godot's documentation says clipped
children are not rendered *and do not receive input*, and
`Viewport::_gui_find_control_at_pos` declines to descend into a clipping control
for an outside point. It was never verified on 4.7 before being written down.
Hiding is retained because it is unambiguous and costs nothing, but the false
mechanism must not be the stated reason.

### 3.7 The doors are visible

Door dwell dominates trip time early on, which is what makes Faster Doors a
strong first purchase — and until now it had **no representation on screen at
all**. A stop looked exactly like standing still, so the upgrade that matters
most in the first hour was something the player had to take on trust.

**A stop is three phases, not one:** the doors slide open, people move, the
doors slide shut. `door_ticks` is the whole stop, and `ElevatorCar` splits a
quarter off each end — never so much that the open window vanishes, because a
stop nobody can board is worse than one with no animation.

**Boarding and alighting happen only in the open window.** That is a rule, not
decoration, and it lives in the sim: `is_available()` excludes both slides. It
was modelled as a single dwell with boarding on the first tick, which was
invisible until the doors were drawn — and then read as passengers stepping
through shut panels.

The view maps those phases to a panel width and owns nothing else. The pairing
is pinned by a test that sweeps **every dwell the upgrade curve can produce and
every tick within it**, asserting the panels are wide open exactly when the car
will accept someone. If the two ever drift apart, that fails.

**The slide is as fast as the doors are** — 0.22 s at level 0, a blink at the
level-8 floor of 4 ticks:

```
1.00s doors:  .....BBBBBBBBBBo.....      . sliding   o open   B boarding
0.20s doors:  .BBo.
```

**The panels are translucent, deliberately.** Opaque doors would hide the seat
rack for the ~95% of the time a car is shut, which is most of when the player
needs to read who is aboard and where they are going. Shut reads as a dimmed
car with its riders still legible; open reads as a bright one.

## 4. Input

### 4.1 Verbs

| Target | Gesture | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built shaft column | vertical drag | dispatch to the floor band under the thumb |
| Built shaft column | tap | dispatch to the floor band tapped |
| Any column | drag past the board edge | cancel |
| Trailing empty shaft slot | tap | buy a shaft |
| Ghost floor band (full width) | tap | buy a floor |
| Vacant floor, x ∈ [0, 240] | tap | open the re-lease confirm (§4.3) |
| Pager `<` `>` | tap | page the shaft strip |
| View button | tap | switch view |

**A tap on a column is a dispatch, not a surge.** Both verbs on a column mean
"send the car there"; a tap is a drag of zero length, resolved against the
*press* point rather than any drift, because below `DRAG_THRESHOLD` the thumb
never committed to a direction. An earlier draft gave tap to surge, which made
the drag the only way to say the obvious thing and spent the cheapest gesture in
the game on a no-op. **Surge therefore has no gesture until it is tuned** —
long-press is the candidate, and unlike double-tap it does not fight mobile
Safari's zoom heuristics. `Gesture.Result.SURGE` and `surge_requested` stay
wired so re-enabling it is a change in one place.

The ghost floor and the trailing shaft slot show their price and grey out when
unaffordable. **Those purchases are a single tap with no confirmation.** The price
is on the target, the target is at least 44pt in both dimensions, and nothing is
destroyed.

### 4.2 Cancel edges and the ghost band

`Gesture` is constructed with `(h, N)` — the row height and the count of **real**
floors — and the shaft column it lives in is `N·h` tall (§3.1). Its existing edge
rule then remains correct: cancel below `-0.5h` or above `h·(N + 0.5)`.

This is why the viewport is inset. Had the columns spanned all `N+1` bands with
`Gesture` still receiving `N`, the cancel edge would fall half a row short of the
board's bottom and **the lower half of the lobby's dispatch band would silently
cancel** — the highest-traffic target in the game. Passing `N+1` instead would
widen the clamp so a ghost-band release resolves to a non-existent floor and
`GameState.dispatch` rejects it, making the drag a silent no-op after the rail
highlighted a target. The inset avoids both.

**Only the top edge is reachable.** The board's bottom is the bottom of the screen,
so cancel-below fires `0.5h` past a boundary no thumb can cross. Cancel is in
practice a drag up into the ghost band or the HUD. The rule stays symmetric because
asymmetry would be a special case with no benefit, but the spec should not claim
two working edges.

### 4.3 Re-leasing is the one confirmed action

A capped row is 29.6 units — **16.16pt** tall. Widening the target to x ∈ [0, 240]
fixes one dimension and not the other, and a vertical miss onto an adjacent
*also-vacant* floor spends $40 on the wrong one.

So the tap opens a confirm anchored beside the row: **"Re-lease floor 12 — $40"**
with 88-unit Confirm and Cancel buttons. This is the single documented exception
to §4.1's no-confirmation rule, and it exists precisely because this is the only
target that cannot meet the 44pt floor. Naming the floor in the prompt also makes
a mis-tap recoverable, which is the actual risk.

Moving re-leasing into Management was the alternative. It was rejected: a vacancy
has a location, and §2's split rule earns its keep only if location-bearing things
stay on the board.

## 5. Management

One scrolling surface: a live readout, then a list under headings — **Spend**,
**Dispatch**, **Prestige**. No tabs. Tabs are a second navigation layer inside a
view already navigated to, and they solve a length problem the list does not yet
have; the list can grow tabs later if it does.

All dynamic text renders through `Label`, or through `RichTextLabel` with
`bbcode_enabled = false`, per the reasoning already applied in `main.gd`: upgrade
levels and costs come from a `user://` save at Milestone 6, on a `github.io`
origin shared with every other Pages site on the account.

### 5.1 The readout

Three numbers over a rolling ~60-simulated-second window:

- **riders / min** — deliveries in the window
- **average wait** — mean seconds a delivered passenger spent waiting on their floor,
  measured spawn-to-boarding but *recorded at delivery*, so a passenger still riding
  is not yet counted. With **zero deliveries in the window it renders `—`**, never
  `0` and never a `NaN` — this is the state a new player sees first.
- **gave up** — expiries in the window

**The window is second-aligned, not exactly 60 seconds.** With 60 one-second
buckets it holds 1200 ticks only at the instant before a rollover and 1181 just
after, so riders/min under-reports by up to 1.58%, oscillating once per second.
Stated rather than hidden, because §5.1's whole claim is that these are true
numbers; per-tick buckets would buy exactness nobody can perceive at 60× the
storage.

**Average wait has survivorship bias, deliberately.** Expiries never enter it, so
as service collapses the average *improves* while `gave up` climbs. The three
numbers are honest jointly, not individually. Recorded so nobody later "fixes" it
by folding expiries in and silently redefines the metric.

**The formatter is a pure function under `sim/`**, not a method on the view, so the
`—` rule and the rounding are testable headlessly without a scene tree.

"% of capacity" was cut. It needs the throughput *ceiling* from §9.1 of the design
spec, which is Milestone 6 machinery that does not exist.

### 5.2 Purchase annotations state mechanical effects, never predicted metrics

A row reads `Faster Doors  Lv2  $60` with `doors 16 → 14 ticks` beneath it — not
`−2s avg wait`. The effect of faster doors on average wait depends on dispatch
policy, traffic phase and crowd size; a predicted delta would be a fabricated
number. The player connects cause to effect by watching the readout move **over
the following minute** — patience runs to 45 s and the window to 60 s, so a
purchase does not move the readout immediately.

**The annotation reads its numbers from `Upgrades`, never from a copy.** The
effect formulas are private to `Upgrades._sync_car()`; duplicating
`maxi(20 - 2·level, 4)` in the view and dropping the clamp would render
`doors 4 → 2 ticks` at Lv8 — precisely what this section forbids. `Upgrades` gains
a query returning the current and next-level value for each effect, and the view
renders only what it returns.

**A zero-delta level is refused by the sim, not just greyed in the view.**
`doors` reaches `DOOR_TICKS_MIN` at level 8 but `max_level` is 12, so levels 8–11
would cost $832, $1,291, $2,001 and $3,101 — **$7,226 for no effect**.
`max_level: 12` stays (lowering `DOOR_TICKS_MIN` later should not require
re-tuning the curve), so `Upgrades.purchase()` itself refuses a level whose effect
value is unchanged, and the view renders it as `doors 4 → 4 ticks (max effect)`
and disabled. Enforcing it only in the view is not enough: two taps queued during
a stalled frame buy level 8 and then level 9 before the button refreshes.

## 6. Simulation additions

All of these live under `sim/` as `RefCounted` and are unit-tested headlessly,
per §8.1 of the design spec.

**The coordinate transform** of §3.2 — the edge table, `floor_to_y`, `car_y`,
`y_to_floor`. Pure functions of `(N, h)`, so there is one definition to change
per era.

**`sim/metrics.gd`** — a ring buffer of 60 one-second buckets (20 ticks each).
Each bucket holds deliveries, summed wait ticks, and expiries. **`Metrics` owns
its own tick counter and never reads `SimClock`**: `clock.note_ticks(1)` runs last
in `_tick_once`, so `ticks_executed` lags the metrics advance for the whole body
of the tick, and deriving the bucket index from it would put every event one
bucket behind.

**The metrics advance is first in the tick order, and the order is written down**
— the house rule `GameState` already states for itself:

```
advance metrics window -> spawn -> move/doors -> deliver -> expire
  -> accrue rent -> update combo
```

Advancing first means the bucket a tick's events land in is the bucket that tick
just rolled into, and no event is written to a bucket about to be cleared.

**`Passenger.waited_ticks()`** — returns `initial_patience - patience_ticks`. No
new field: patience decays only while waiting and is frozen once aboard, so at
delivery this is exactly the ticks spent waiting. **Its contract is defined, not
undefined:** `_initial_patience` is `maxi(patience, 1)`, so for a passenger
constructed with patience ≤ 0 the value is high by one. The function clamps at
zero and the contract is stated as *meaningful for patience ≥ 1*; the spawner
clamps `base_patience_ticks` to ≥ 1 so no production passenger can violate it, and
the test asserts the clamped result rather than leaving it to discovery.

**`GameState.relet(row) -> bool`** — new command. Today `Tenancy.relet()` restores
a tenant without touching `Economy`, and `Tenancy.relet_cost()` has **no production
caller anywhere in the repo** — only tests. Wiring §4's tap straight to
`tenancy.relet(row)` makes re-leasing free forever and hollows out the rule it is
balanced against: §5.3's no-fail guarantee is that re-leasing is free
*conditionally*. The command validates the row is vacant, reads `relet_cost(row)`,
spends through `Economy`, and only then relets — returning false without charging
if any step fails. **The order matters:** `relet_cost` is derived from
`tenanted_count()`, so reletting first would turn a free re-lease into a $40 one.

**`Upgrades` effect query and zero-delta refusal** — the query returns current and
next-level values for each code-defined effect; `purchase()` refuses when the next
value equals the current one (§5.2).

## 8. Verification

**Unit tests** (GUT, headless):

1. `Metrics`: empty window, partial window, rollover at 1200 ticks, a window
   wrapped more than once, and **an event recorded on the rollover tick itself** —
   a delivery on tick 1200 is counted, and still counted at tick 1219.
2. The readout formatter (a pure function, §5.1): zero deliveries renders `—`.
3. `waited_ticks()` for a passenger delivered on the tick it reaches zero patience
   (the §8.3 boundary), **and** the stated clamped result for patience ≤ 0.
4. The transform: `y_to_floor(floor_to_y(f)) == f` for every f at **every**
   N ∈ [1, 40] — exact by construction with the edge table, so this is a real
   assertion rather than a float coin-flip — plus band-centre round trips,
   monotonicity, and `car_y` continuity at 0.0, 0.5 and N−1.
5. `Gesture` cancel edges against the ghost band: a release anywhere inside floor
   0's band dispatches and never cancels, parameterised over N = 6, 28, 29, 39, 40.
6. `GameState.relet`: cash falls by `RELET_COST`; free when zero rows are tenanted;
   refused, and nothing charged, when unaffordable or the row is not vacant.
7. The density tier function: `1184/(N+G) ≤ 40 ⟺ N ≥ 29`, as arithmetic rather than
   a screenshot, so it survives a change to `HUD_HEIGHT`.
8. `Upgrades`: the effect query at doors Lv7→8 reports a change, Lv8→9 reports none,
   and `purchase()` **refuses and does not charge** at Lv8→9.
9. The tick-T/T+1 boundary that spec §8.3 already requires and no test pins.

**Driven against the running game.** These need `tests/test_board_input.gd`, a
harness that **does not exist yet** — it instantiates the scene and injects
synthetic input. An earlier draft cited "the paging regression" as precedent; that
was a throwaway probe surviving only in a commit message, and the plan must build
the harness rather than reuse it.

10. A drag onto the band showing floor N's label dispatches to floor N — the check
    that catches a mirrored board, which no screenshot can.
11. The car and the rail marker agree with the floor label — the two surfaces that
    mirror *self-consistently* and pass casual play.
12. A tap at x = 400 in the ghost band buys a floor and does not surge.
13. A drag from a column up into the ghost band: `y ∈ [−0.5h, 0)` selects the top
    floor, `y < −0.5h` cancels.
14. A drag over the people strip while paged dispatches nothing; a drag on the
    leftmost visible column commands that column's shaft, not shaft 0.
15. Every shaft from 1 to `MAX_SHAFTS` is reachable: from `owned` shafts a buyable
    slot is visible or reachable by paging, for every `owned` < 8; and a tap on a
    non-trailing placeholder does nothing.
16. The re-lease span: a tap at x = 300 on a vacant floor reaches the column, not
    the re-lease confirm; a tap at x = 100 on a tenanted floor does nothing.

**Screenshots.** The board at **6 and 40 floors × 1 and 8 shafts**, plus 28 and 29
floors as the two sides of the density tier. Reaching 40 floors by play costs
~$1.36×10⁸, so the matrix needs a **debug override** — a command-line flag or debug
scene that injects the starting `GameState`. It must not be done by editing
`START_ROWS` / `START_SHAFTS`, because an unreverted edit ships every new player a
40-floor building.

## 9. Deltas against the 2026-08-01 design spec

Behavioural:

- **§3 "the board never scrolls"** becomes horizontal-only: the shaft strip pages,
  the floors never scroll. The 8-shaft cap no longer follows from "8.9 columns fit
  edge to edge" — retained as a design choice, and a later era could raise it.
- **§2.1 detents** are floor *bands*, not the nearest multiple of row height.
- **§5.3 re-leasing** acquires a UI, a confirm step, and for the first time a code
  path that actually charges for it.
- **§8.5 crowd bar**: the trigger changes from `waiting > 12` to `row_height ≤ 40`,
  and sprites and bars become mutually exclusive.

Edits that design spec needs, which this document does not itself make:

- **§2.1** derives everything from "a row is exactly 32 units… roughly 17.5 pt" at
  1280. The board is 1184, so a capped row is **29.6 units / 16.16 pt** and half a
  row is **14.8**, not 16. `DRAG_THRESHOLD = 12` still satisfies the invariant with
  2.8 units of margin, but the bound is wrong in four places: §2.1, §13, the comment
  in `sim/gesture.gd`, and `tests/test_gesture.gd`'s `assert_lt(…, 16.0)`.
- **§3** — the 8.9-columns derivation, and the same derivation in `sim/building.gd`.
- **§8.2** — the `ui/` list still names `upgrade_panel`; `sim/` has no `metrics` or
  coordinate-transform entry.
- **§8.3** — "A passenger spawned on tick T first decays on tick T+1. Both
  boundaries are pinned by test." The second sentence is **false**: no test pins
  either boundary. The code also decays on tick T, since `_tick_once` spawns and
  then expires in the same call. One tick in a ~900-tick budget, so no number here
  moves — but one of the two documents is wrong and §8 test 9 now covers it.
- **§8.5** — the node budget counts 1,440 passenger nodes *and* 80 crowd bars as
  coexisting. Under §3.5 they are exclusive, and the sprite tier caps at N=28, so
  the passenger term is `28×12×3 = 1,008` and the peak subtotal is **~1,124 at
  N=28** against the stated 1,672 — a ~33% drop, not the ~5% "minus 80" implies.
  The "measured on the target iPhone" budget should be re-baselined against it.

## 10. Out of scope

Juice and animation beyond what exists. Tenant names on the board. Surge magnitude
and cooldown. Everything in Milestones 4–7 beyond leaving room for it in the
Management list.

## 11. Open items

- The 40-unit density threshold is a legibility guess above the geometric floor,
  and should be checked on the device.
- Whether the crowd bar should encode anything beyond worst-patience colour and
  crowd length is unexplored.
- Whether `clip_contents` blocks input on Godot 4.7 specifically is unverified;
  one probe would let §3.6's hide be justified as redundancy rather than necessity.
- Cancel-below is unreachable (§4.2). Whether Godot routes post-press motion to the
  pressed control beyond its own rect is assumed, not verified.
- **Board purchases make a mid-drag rebuild reachable.** Buying a floor or shaft
  calls `rebuild()`, which frees every column. Purchases used to live on a sheet
  that covered the board; now a second thumb can buy from the ghost band while the
  first is mid-dispatch, killing the drag silently. Device testing should look for it.
- The region widths, `visible_shafts`, and `MAX_SHAFTS` are literals in prose here;
  the implementation plan should name them as constants in one place.
