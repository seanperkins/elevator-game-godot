# UI Design — Board and Management

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Status:** Approved. Implementation plan is the next artifact.
**Supersedes nothing.** Extends `2026-08-01-elevator-incremental-design.md`; §11 of that
document lists the deltas this one introduces.

## 1. What this covers

The screen, at both ends of a run: 6 floors and one shaft, and 40 floors and eight.
It settles where information lives, what each gesture does, and how the layout
degrades as the board fills. It does not touch game rules.

Three comparable games shaped it. **SimTower** made elevator management the whole
game and became unwieldy at scale — the failure this design is arranged against.
**Project Highrise** solved that by deleting the mechanic, which is the trap.
**Tiny Tower** is the mobile reference, and its consistent complaint is menu
clutter, which is why Management is one surface rather than a tree. **Elevator
Saga** scores the player on throughput and wait time; those numbers are what
Management leads with. **Mini Metro** is the posture: the board is the UI.

## 2. Two views

**Board** — the building. Dispatch, and every purchase that has a physical place.
**Management** — everything with no place on the board: invisible upgrades,
dispatch policy, prestige, and the numbers that justify them.

A `MANAGE` button in the HUD switches between them. The simulation runs in both.

The button sits top-right, which is the worst reach on the phone. That is
deliberate: a floating button in the thumb zone would overlay the bottom-right of
the board, and at eight shafts the bottom-right of the board *is* a shaft column
— a dispatch target. A permanent tab bar would cost 88 units of board height for
a control used a few times a minute. Top-right is the cheapest correct place.

## 3. The board

### 3.1 Geometry

720x1280 portrait. A 96-unit HUD holds cash, rent/min, combo, the shaft pager and
`MANAGE`. The remaining **1184 units are board**.

Floors run **bottom-up**: the lobby is floor 0 at the bottom, and floor *i* draws
at `board_bottom - (i + 1) * row_height`. The pre-existing build drew floor 0 at
the top and counted down the screen, which is upside down for a building.

Row height is `1184 / (floors + 1)` while the building is under the 40-floor cap,
and `1184 / 40` at the cap. The `+ 1` is the ghost floor (§3.3).

### 3.2 Row anatomy

Four fixed regions, identical on every floor at every board size:

| x range | width | contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–26 | 26 | waiting count, right-aligned, brightest element on the row |
| 30–34 | 4 | satisfaction bar, red→green |
| 38–64 | 26 | floor number, dimmed |
| 64–240 | 176 | people strip |
| 240–720 | 480 | shaft viewport: five 96-unit columns |

**The count is leftmost and never moves.** It is the number a dispatch decision is
made on, so it cannot be the element whose position depends on how many shafts the
player owns, and it cannot sit unlabelled beside the floor number where the two
read as one value.

The people strip is a **fixed** 176 units — twelve sprites at a 14-unit pitch plus
margin. Sizing it from whatever the columns leave over means it reaches zero at
the shaft cap, which deletes the crowd exactly when the building is busiest.

96-unit columns are 52pt at the 0.546 iPhone 15 scale, comfortably over the 44pt
floor from §2.1. Five fit in the viewport; the rest are reached with the pager
(§3.5).

### 3.3 The ghost floor

While the building is under 40 floors, a full-height empty row sits above the top
floor reading `+ BUILD FLOOR $<cost>`. Tapping it buys a floor.

It is a row, not a button, so the next floor is always visibly *there* waiting to
be bought, and the affordance disappears at the cap without a special case: at 40
floors the term simply leaves the row-height divisor.

### 3.4 Density tiers

Above a **40-unit row height** the people strip draws individual sprites, coloured
green→red by remaining patience, capped at 12 with the count carrying the
overflow.

At or below 40 units the sprites collapse into a **single crowd bar**: its colour
is the *worst* patience on that floor, and its length is the crowd measured against
the same 12-passenger cap, saturating at full width beyond that. A floor with 12
waiting and one with 40 therefore draw the same bar — the count beside it is what
distinguishes them, and the count is always exact.

With the ghost floor in the divisor the tier changes at 29 floors. The threshold
is a tunable constant, not a derived law.

The alternative — keeping rows tall enough for sprites and scrolling the board
vertically — was rejected. Dispatch is an absolute drag onto a floor's band, so a
vertically scrolled board can only dispatch to floors currently on screen, and
"any floor is one short drag away" is the property §2.1's whole input model rests
on. **The board never scrolls vertically.**

### 3.5 Shaft paging

The shaft viewport shows five columns and pages horizontally with `<` `>` controls
in the HUD, which also report `shafts 4-8 of 8`. The pager hides itself entirely
while every shaft fits. Buying a shaft pages to it.

Paging is a **tap, never a swipe**. §2.1 establishes that a vertical thumb drag
arcs sideways by more than half a column, so any horizontal reading of motion on
the board would steal the primary verb.

Columns paged out of view are **hidden, not merely clipped**: `clip_contents`
suppresses drawing but not hit-testing, and a column scrolled off the left lands
underneath the people strip, where a drag would dispatch a shaft the player cannot
see.

## 4. Input

Every verb has a distinct spatial target, so none is disambiguated by timing.

| Target | Gesture | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built shaft column | vertical drag | dispatch to the floor band under the thumb |
| Built shaft column | tap | surge (a no-op until tuned) |
| Any column | drag past the board edge | cancel |
| Empty shaft slot | tap | buy a shaft |
| Ghost floor | tap | buy a floor |
| Vacant floor's gutter | tap | re-lease that floor |
| Pager `<` `>` | tap | page the shaft strip |
| `MANAGE` | tap | switch view |

Empty slots and the ghost floor show their price and grey out when unaffordable.
**A purchase is a single tap with no confirmation step.** The price is on the
target and nothing is destroyed, so the worst case is spending money a moment
early. Arm-then-confirm is the safer alternative and is deliberately not taken.

Re-leasing is a board interaction because a vacancy has a location. It stays free
whenever the player holds zero tenanted rows, per §5.3 of the design spec — the
single rule that makes the no-fail guarantee hold.

## 5. Management

One scrolling surface: a live readout, then a list under headings — **Spend**,
**Dispatch**, **Prestige**. No tabs. Tabs are a second navigation layer inside a
view already navigated to, and they solve a length problem the list does not yet
have; the list can grow tabs later if it does.

### 5.1 The readout

Three numbers over a rolling 60-simulated-second window:

- **riders / min** — deliveries in the window
- **average wait** — mean seconds a delivered passenger spent waiting on their floor,
  measured spawn-to-boarding but *recorded at delivery*, so a passenger still riding
  is not yet counted
- **gave up** — expiries in the window

"% of capacity" was cut. It needs the throughput *ceiling* from §9.1 of the design
spec, which is Milestone 6 machinery that does not exist. Three true numbers beat
four with one bluffing.

### 5.2 Purchase annotations state mechanical effects, never predicted metrics

A row reads `Faster Doors  Lv2  $60` with `doors 16 → 14 ticks` beneath it — not
`−2s avg wait`. The effect of faster doors on average wait depends on dispatch
policy, traffic phase and crowd size; a predicted delta would be a fabricated
number. The player connects cause to effect by watching the readout move, which is
also what makes the readout worth having.

## 6. Simulation additions

Both live under `sim/` as `RefCounted` and are unit-tested headlessly, per §8.1.

**`sim/metrics.gd`** — a ring buffer of 60 one-second buckets (20 ticks each,
1200 ticks total). Each bucket holds deliveries, summed wait ticks, and expiries.
`GameState` advances it one tick at a time and reports deliveries and expiries into
it. Queries return the window totals.

**`Passenger.waited_ticks()`** — returns `initial_patience - patience_ticks`. No
new field: patience decays only while a passenger is waiting and is frozen once
aboard, so at delivery this is exactly the ticks spent waiting.

## 7. Code impact

| File | Change |
| --- | --- |
| `sim/metrics.gd` | new |
| `sim/passenger.gd` | `waited_ticks()` |
| `sim/game_state.gd` | own metrics; report deliveries/expiries; advance the window |
| `view/floor_row.gd` | bottom-up orientation, region rewrite, crowd-bar tier |
| `view/building_view.gd` | bottom-up layout, ghost floor, tappable empty slots, re-lease tap |
| `ui/management_view.gd` | replaces `ui/upgrade_panel.gd`: readout plus sectioned list |
| `game/game_root.gd` | view switching, pager, HUD |

`data/upgrades.json` is unchanged. "New Shaft" and "Build a Floor" leave the
Management list but remain `Upgrades` entries with their cost curves and caps
intact — only the trigger moves to the board, so the existing board-cap tests keep
passing untouched.

## 8. Verification

- `Metrics` gets its own GUT suite: empty window, partial window, exact rollover at
  1200 ticks, and a window that has wrapped more than once.
- `waited_ticks()` is pinned by test for a passenger delivered on the tick it
  reaches zero patience — the §8.3 boundary.
- The board is screenshotted from the running game at **6, 28, 29 and 40 floors**.
  28 and 29 are the two sides of the density tier and must be checked as a pair.
- A drag over the people strip while the shaft strip is paged must dispatch
  nothing; a drag on the leftmost visible column must command that column's shaft,
  not shaft 0. This held when paging was built, but the probe that proved it was a
  throwaway and no longer exists, and this design moves every region on the row —
  so it must be re-driven against the rebuilt board, not assumed.

## 9. Deltas against the 2026-08-01 design spec

- **§3 "the board never scrolls"** is now horizontal-only in effect: the shaft
  strip pages, the floors never scroll. The 8-shaft cap therefore no longer follows
  from "8.9 columns fit edge to edge" — it is retained as a design choice, and a
  later era could raise it without a layout crisis.
- **§2.1 detents** are floor *bands*, not the nearest multiple of row height. A
  detent on the row's top edge puts the rail highlight half a row from what release
  dispatches.
- **§5.3 re-leasing** acquires a UI: a tap on a vacant floor's gutter.
- **§8.5 sprite cap** gains a second tier — the crowd bar — below a 40-unit row.

## 10. Out of scope

Juice and animation beyond what exists. Tenant names on the board: the Ledger
layout won, and names were the competing layout's argument. Surge magnitude and
cooldown. Everything in Milestones 4–7 beyond leaving room for it in the
Management list.

## 11. Open items

- The 40-unit density threshold is a guess that should be checked on the device.
- Whether the crowd bar should encode anything beyond worst-patience colour and
  crowd length — average patience, or direction of travel — is unexplored.
- Purchase confirmation: single-tap is chosen; revisit if device testing shows
  accidental buys.
